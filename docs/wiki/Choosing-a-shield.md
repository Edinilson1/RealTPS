# Choosing a shield

[← Wiki home](Home.md)

Which material to take on which mission. The details of each material are in [Materials](Materials.md). Treat
these as starting points: they come from the mod's numerical model of stock Kerbin entries and from my own
flights, so test a new mission before you trust a crew to it.

## By mission

| Mission | Good choices | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Suborbital hop | Anything; cork-phenolic is the cheapest | — |
| Return from low orbit, capsule | Cork-phenolic (cheap), phenolic-fiberglass, Avcoat; PICA or C-PICA to save mass | Silica tiles |
| Return from low orbit, light craft on a large shield, reusable | TUFROC, reinforced carbon-carbon; UHTC on the small shields | Silica tiles on anything but very gentle entries |
| Return from the Mun or Minmus | Avcoat, carbon-phenolic, HEEET; PICA or C-PICA for light craft; phenolic-fiberglass on steep returns only | Cork, SLA-561V, SIRCA, all reusable surfaces, the blankets |
| Return from another planet (Duna, Mars in Real Solar System) | Carbon-phenolic or HEEET for margin; Avcoat for a moderate mass | Cork, PICA and C-PICA on heavy capsules, and everything above |
| Landing on Duna or Mars | SLA-561V (what the real Mars landers used), PICA; the inflatable shield for heavy landers | — |
| Large, light payload from low orbit | The inflatable shield with a blanket | — |
| Faster entries with the inflatable shield | ADEPT fabric | The blankets |
| Making History pods | Cork for low orbit; Avcoat or C-PICA for low orbit and steep Mun returns | Long Mun returns on the pod's own coating: put a heat shield under it |

## Rules of thumb

- **More shield area per tonne means less heat per square metre.** A heavy capsule on a small shield is the worst
  case. The same material on a bigger shield, or under a lighter craft, lasts a lot longer.
- **Steep or shallow.** A steep entry is short and violent: the surface gets very hot, but the total heat is
  lower. A shallow one is gentle but long: you use more material, and the heat has time to reach the bond.
  Ablators with a thin layer suffer the most on long, shallow entries.
- **Keep the shield facing the flow.** If it's tilted, the windward side wears out first and can be breached while
  the rest is still fine.
- **Two entries, one shield.** If the same shield does an aerobrake and then the landing, or a landing and then
  the return, whatever you burned in the first one is gone for the second. Take a material with plenty to spare,
  or a shield for each.
- **Mass matters.** Carbon-phenolic and HEEET survive almost anything, but they're heavy. For a low-orbit capsule,
  cork-phenolic or Avcoat is the better trade.
- **Reusable means gentle.** Tiles, TUFROC, RCC and UHTC are for light vehicles with a lot of shield area. On a
  dense capsule they fail by temperature, no matter how much shield is left.
- **Real Solar System:** entries are longer and hotter than on Kerbin. In my tests, cork-phenolic on a 2.5 m
  shield survived a Moon return with only 6% left. Test before you trust it.
